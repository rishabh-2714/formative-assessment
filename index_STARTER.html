Elephant (Family: Elephantidae)

About my Animal

Elephants are the largest living land animals. Three living species are currently recognised: the African bush elephant (Loxodonta africana), the African forest elephant (L. cyclotis), and the Asian elephant (Elephas maximus). They are the only surviving members of the family Elephantidae and the order Proboscidea.

Distinctive features of elephants include a long proboscis called a trunk, tusks, large ear flaps, pillar-like legs, and tough but sensitive grey skin. The trunk is prehensile, bringing food and water to the mouth and grasping objects. Tusks, which are derived from the incisor teeth, serve both as weapons and as tools for moving objects and digging. The large ear flaps assist in maintaining a constant body temperature as well as in communication. African elephants have larger ears and concave backs, whereas Asian elephants have smaller ears and convex or level backs.

Routine

  1. They need to consume 300kg of vegetation and 200L of water a day, meaning that they roam in search of food and water for 18 hours, 80% of their day
  2. They sleep while lying down, for about 3-4 hours a day.

Physical Adaptations A long, muscular trunk is used to easily drink water from its height, two tusks help in digging, foraging and self-defence, large ears help to cool oneself, and large, high-nerve feet with padding prevent the elephant fromgetting hurt while allowing it to sense seismic vibrations from far away.
Relatives and Classification There are three living species of elephant (African bush, African forest, and Asian). However, the mammoth and mastodon are some extinct relatives. The elephant is classified as a warm-blooded elephantid animal, under the two genera of Elephas and Loxodonta.
Diet and Habitat The average adult elephant consumes 150 to 300 kg of vegetation and 100 to 200 L of water per day, spending up to 18 hours!
Their feces disperses seeds throughout their habitat, which is usually a tropical forest or savannah.
Social Structure and Reproduction Elephant herds are led by an elderly female matriarch in wandering their forest or savannah. They are inherently peaceful and do not aim to hurt any other species. Their males are called elephant bulls and their females are called elephant cows. They have a 22-month gestation period, compensated by the fact that their calves can stand up within minutes and keep up with the walking pace within days.
